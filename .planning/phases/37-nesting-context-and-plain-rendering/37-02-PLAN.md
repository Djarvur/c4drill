<objective>
CTX-02: when a link's true target lies under a depicted ancestor but is not itself depicted, the view generator adds the target's ancestor chain (from the depicted ancestor down to the target) as visible entries and resolves the edge to the TRUE target — the deep-link target keeps its container context instead of silently collapsing to an anonymous top-level ancestor.

Purpose: `resolveBoundaryLink` (internal/view/scope.go L308-351) resolves every link peer via `resolveToTopLevel` to the nearest VISIBLE ancestor, so a link to `linuxSystem.sshAuth.sshd` in C1 becomes an edge to `linuxSystem` with no trace of the deeper target. The collapse was a deliberate anti-pollution choice — CTX-02 reverses it for link TARGETS only (sibling/external boundary nodes stay top-level per RESEARCH scoping).

Output: Chain insertion in `resolveBoundaryLink` (C1) and `addResolvedCrossLink`/`addResolvedCrossLinkFrom` (C2/C3), deterministic ordering of new entries, and scope tests (RED first).

Scope guard (RESEARCH, Out of Scope): container chains render only for DEPICTED link targets reached by this mechanism; collapsed (non-depicted) subtrees are NOT restructured wholesale. CTX-01 (the invariant that every depicted element sits inside its chain) is proven in plan 37-05 once this plan and plan 37-01 (recursive clusters that render the chains) have both landed.

Known golden impact (BY DESIGN): C1/C2/C3 output changes for models with deep links (multilevel.toml among them). Committed-golden baseline failures after GREEN are the documented CTX-02 delta — re-baselined in ONE consolidated task in plan 37-05. Do NOT re-baseline goldens here.
</objective>
